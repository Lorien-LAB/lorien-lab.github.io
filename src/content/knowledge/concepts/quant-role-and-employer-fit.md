## Core Idea

Role fit is a hypothesis about work, not a ranking of titles. Separate the function performed from the employer environment, compare both with the same evidence, and revise the hypothesis when daily-work evidence changes.

The role-function axis describes the work product, the research-engineering balance, proximity to decisions, expected time horizon, and the skills that transfer to adjacent work. The employer-environment axis describes how decisions are made, how risk is owned, how quickly feedback arrives, how much ambiguity is tolerated, and which constraints shape the work. Keeping the axes separate makes a preference clear without pretending it is permanent.

## Map the Role Function

Describe pricing and trading support, model validation and risk, quantitative research, quantitative development, systematic research and trading, and portfolio or capital modeling by their principal work product and responsibility boundary. For each role, ask what is produced, who uses it, how much research and engineering it requires, and how close it is to a consequential decision.

Record the normal time horizon, feedback cycle, pace, ambiguity, risk ownership, and transferable skills. A useful map distinguishes building a model, testing a model, maintaining production systems, explaining exposures, and supporting a portfolio decision even when job titles overlap.

## Map the Employer Environment

Compare banks and sell-side institutions; hedge funds, market makers, and proprietary trading firms; asset managers; consulting, audit, and validation organizations; and financial-software or financial-technology companies without ranking them universally.

For each environment, inspect the work product it rewards, its research-engineering balance, decision proximity, time horizon, feedback cycle, pace, ambiguity, and risk ownership. Note how the setting affects collaboration, review, incentives, controls, and the kinds of transferable skills that are developed. Treat these as tendencies to verify, not guarantees attached to an institution type.

## Compare with One Lens

Use work product, research-engineering balance, decision proximity, time horizon, feedback cycle, pace, ambiguity, risk ownership, and transferable skills as the common comparison dimensions. Apply every dimension to both candidate role functions and candidate employer environments before drawing a conclusion.

Turn each dimension into observable evidence: a representative deliverable for work product, time allocation for research versus engineering, the person or process receiving an output, the forecast or holding period, how quickly outcomes can be evaluated, the operating tempo, the number of underspecified choices, the consequences owned, and which methods remain useful in another setting.

## Build a Fit Hypothesis

1. Identify the problems you want to solve.
2. Describe the daily work and output you want.
3. Compare role function independently from employer environment.
4. Gather evidence from job descriptions, conversations, and representative tasks.
5. State a provisional preference and revise it when evidence changes.

Make the hypothesis specific enough to test: name the work product, the preferred research-engineering balance, the decision distance, the time horizon, and the level of risk ownership. Mark unknowns explicitly, then update the view when observed work contradicts an assumption. A revisable preference signals judgment rather than indecision.

## Common Mistakes

Reject title-only matching, prestige rankings, universal compensation assumptions, and treating one institution’s organization chart as an industry-wide taxonomy. Also avoid inferring daily work from a single job description, collapsing role function and employer environment into one label, or treating a preferred pace or time horizon as objectively better.

## Interview Checks

1. Why must role function and employer environment be evaluated separately?
2. Compare two role functions using the common lens rather than title or prestige.
3. How can the same title describe different work in two institutions?
4. How would you communicate a reasoned but revisable role preference to an interviewer?
